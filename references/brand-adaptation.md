# Brand adaptation

Apple-inspired does not mean Apple-branded. The system should carry the product’s identity while borrowing Apple’s clarity, restraint, and care.

## Brand inputs

Use the user’s provided brand assets and preferences for logo, accent, type, imagery, tone, and density. Ask before changing an established identity. If no brand exists, use a neutral foundation and one restrained accent.

## Color

Map brand colors to semantic roles instead of painting every component with the brand hue. Derive hover, pressed, soft, focus, inverse, and dark values in a perceptual space and validate contrast. Keep success, warning, danger, and informational meanings independent from the brand accent.

## Type and tone

Use the platform’s readable system type when appropriate, then express personality through scale, weight, spacing, and copy. Do not force SF Pro, Inter, a serif, or a display face when the product’s audience or language needs something else.

Write in the product’s language and voice. Keep labels concrete, errors helpful, and headings focused on the user’s outcome.

## Signature

Choose at most one or two signature decisions per surface: a distinctive hero composition, a meaningful motion moment, a characteristic shape, or a deliberate image treatment. Let the rest stay quiet so the signature is memorable.
