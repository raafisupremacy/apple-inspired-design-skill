# Presentations and email

## Keynote-style presentations

Apple keynotes are known for one idea per slide, very few words, big imagery, and consistent type.

- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs bullets, it is probably several slides. Reveal sequences over multiple builds or slides.
- **Few words, huge type.** A statement slide may carry 1–7 words at 80–140pt. Body text, when needed, ≥ 28pt.
- **One emphasized object** per slide: a number, an image, a quote, a product.
- **Grounds:** dark or brand-colored grounds make images and type glow; light grounds for data-heavy decks. Choose with the user's color direction and keep it consistent.
- **Numbers as heroes:** "2×" or "18 hours" at giant size with a small label underneath; source in a footnote.
- **Images full-bleed** or a product on a clean ground with soft light.
- **Continuity:** keep objects in the same place between slides and animate between them (Magic Move style) to show change.
- **Charts simplified:** one message per chart, highlight the relevant bar/line in the accent, everything else muted, direct labels.

Slide types: title, statement, big number, image full-bleed, product on stage, comparison (two columns), simple chart, quote, demo cue, section divider, closing CTA.

Specs: 16:9 at 1920×1080; safe margins ≥ 80px; system font or the brand font (embed or outline when sharing); test readability from the back of a room or at small video size.

## Email

- Single column 600–640px, fluid on mobile; live HTML text for headline and body (not text baked into images).
- Structure: logo small → hero image with alt text → headline (2–6 words) → one sentence → one bulletproof CTA button → optional supporting blocks → footer with address and unsubscribe.
- System font stack; 16px+ body; 44px tall buttons.
- Dark mode: declare `color-scheme` / `supported-color-schemes` meta, use transparent logos with a subtle outline or light variant, avoid pure black text on images that clients may invert, and test in major clients.
- Color: brand on the CTA and one accent element; tinted ground blocks to separate sections.
- Accessibility: real headings, alt text, sufficient contrast, readable without images.
